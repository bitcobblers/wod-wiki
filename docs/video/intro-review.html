wod.wiki Explainer Video — Review

Scope: docs/video/ (outline, production rules, characters, asset guide, scenes 1–7) · Checked against the repository on 2026-10-21

Verdict: the video is buildable and unusually well-disciplined — every on-screen number, the exact Tabata text, the fence syntax, the fragment order, the WQL query shape, and the storage story all check out against the repo. Three claims oversell the current build (notebook-level GitHub publish, authored METs, cast hardware), and the documents contradict each other in four small places. Nothing requires re-shooting the story; all fixes are copy- or spec-level.

1 · Intent — what this intro is trying to do

The outline states the product story in one line: you write markdown → the markdown logs metrics or becomes a timer that logs them → you query the results with WQL in dashboards, pitched as “Obsidian meets a gym timer meets Datadog.” Checked against the repo, that one-liner is accurate — it is a fair compression of the markdown/canvas note model (markdown/canvas/), the timer runtime (markdown/canvas/guide/protocols.md, packages/lang/src/runtime/), and WQL (docs/language/wql-reference.md).

The seven scenes form a single argument, one clause per beat:

1 · Pain: fragmented tools→ 2 · Relief: it’s just text→ 3 · Aha: a line declares its metrics→ 4 · Proof: execution is the logging→ 5 · Context: the home gym→ 6 · Payoff: results → answers→ 7 · Trust + CTA: yours, and shared

The intent is conversion, not education: get a first-time visitor from “what is this?” to “let me try it” in ~90 seconds and hand them to the live demo below the fold. Every scene serves exactly one clause of the one-liner; nothing is orphaned, and there is no feature tourism. That is the right intent for a landing-page asset.

Findings — where the video and the repo disagree

A · Oversell “Publish a notebook to GitHub” is not a shipped action

Scene 7 shows a one-click Publish button (GitHub mark) on your own notebook card. The repo’s publishing path is repo-level, not notebook-level: a markdown repository is compiled with npx wod build into manifest/chunk files and deployed to static hosting / GitHub Pages (docs/architecture/interfaces.md:129, docs/prototypes/seed-data-unification.md:111). Subscription is real but sits at the seed/source level — one Subscribe imports every notebook a seed publishes (docs/adrs/0002-catalog-source-lifecycle.md:27). No notebook-level publish action exists in the app or CLI.

Fix: reword to “Publish your training library on GitHub — anyone can subscribe” (matches reality), or gate the button shot on actually shipping notebook-level publish.

B · Overpromise “calories, METs” as things a line can carry

Scene 3’s narration and the metric chips claim a statement can carry calories and METs. Repo reality: cal/kcal are registered units, but a line like 20 cal Row fuses into a generic MeasuredMetric (energy dimension) via packages/lang/src/runtime/compiler/metrics/dimensionFactory.ts — there is no first-class calorie metric type, and the WQL metric-key list (docs/language/wql-reference.md) has no calories key. METs cannot be authored on a line at all: METScore ('met-score') is a derived analytics metric computed from effort-registry MET values (packages/core/src/models/Metric.ts). The chip row promises queryability the parser and WQL don’t deliver.

Fix: chips become reps · load · distance · time · calories (all real), and the narration drops METs — or keep METs only if the effort registry is credited as their source.

C · Shipped-vs-pending Cast / TV remote / Bluetooth keyboard presented as done

Scene 5 sells Chromecast, TV-remote splits, and Bluetooth keyboards as current features. docs/clock-command-controls.md:3 says this is implemented in the clock-command-board worktree but “Physical Chromecast, Bluetooth keypad and mobile OS delivery still require hardware acceptance,” and line 36 explicitly disclaims any exercised end-to-end claim. The cast receiver code exists (apps/playground/src/app/cast/); the hardware proof does not.

Fix: acceptable to keep if the team accepts the risk; otherwise soften to “built for the big screen” with the cast beat implied, not demonstrated. Decide before the voice session.

D · Internal contradiction Metric chip count: 6 vs 5

00-asset-guide.md fixes the chips at six: reps · load · distance · time · calories · METs (“in VO order”). Scene 3’s on-screen text and animation spec build a row of five — no time. Since the asset guide declares itself “the video’s single source of truth,” one of them is wrong. (Finding B may shrink both.)

E · Internal contradiction Scene 5 lands R5; the asset guide says R5–R6

The asset guide’s dataset section says “Scene 5 continues R5–R6.” Scene 5’s spec lands only R5 · 12; R6 never lands anywhere — Scene 6 cuts to the full table. Harmless on screen, but the continuity docs should agree since the checklist explicitly audits number provenance.

F · Pacing math “90-second cut” is a 94-second read with zero air

The outline claims 235 words ≈ 90 seconds and “the assembled timeline is 1:33, with the extra seconds as air between scenes.” At the character sheet’s locked ~150 wpm, 235 words is 235 / 150 × 60 = 94s of narration against exactly 93s of timeline (per-scene slots sum to 1:33). There is no air — narration and picture are back-to-back at 101% density, and several scenes spec simultaneous VO + typing + animation. Also, scene files mix time conventions: Scene 1’s “annotation at 0:11” is scene-local while its own spec places it at scene-local 0:12; Scene 2’s “at 0:21” is timeline time. The docs never define which clock a spec line uses.

Fix: cut ~6–8 words (Scene 3 is the densest at 41 words/16s) or accept a 1:34 cut and drop the “air” claim; declare one time convention (scene-local) in production rules.

G · Broken cross-references Outline points at folders that don’t exist

The outline’s “Companion documents” section references scenes/scene-01…07-*.md, reference/characters.md, reference/ui-and-asset-guide.md, reference/production-rules.md. Actual layout is flat: docs/video/scene-0N-*.md, docs/video/00-*.md. Production rules also say renders go “beside the scene’s .md file in scenes/” — there is no scenes/ directory. Every link in the video docs’ own index is dead as written.

H · Presentation simplification “It’s in your journal… under the original block”

Scene 6 / UI-3 place the results table on the same page as the original block, in the journal. The repo renders session results on the Session Detail route (/sessions/:id, RowsTable in docs/screens/session-detail.md:13); the journal is a note stream, not a per-session results view. The video must either trace Session Detail as UI-3, or an in-note results table needs to ship first — per the asset guide’s own Rule #1 (“traced, don’t invent”), the current UI-3 spec cannot be traced from the build.

I · Continuity rule violation Scene 3 uses four marker annotations, max is three

Production rules cap marker ink at “max 3 per scene” (the only Scene 3 exception granted is a second camera move). Scene 3’s spec draws four bracket annotations: “the clock,” “the effort,” “the load,” “the distance.” Either fold “the load”/“the distance” into the app-styled highlight (no ink), or amend the rule.

J · Nit Scene 1’s “rule bent once” note is wrong

“three apps, three formats” is four words — the ≤5-word rule isn’t bent at all. The parenthetical erodes confidence in the constraint tracking for zero benefit.

3 · Cohesiveness — what holds the film together

The package is cohesive to an unusual degree for pre-production docs. The mechanisms doing the work:

  • One dataset (T-1) end to end. The same Tabata is typed in Scene 2, dissected in Scene 3, executed in Scene 4, continued in Scene 5, tabulated and charted in Scene 6, published in Scene 7. Numbers never re-invented; total (100) is arithmetically sound. This is the single strongest consistency device in the package.
  • One continuous shot across Scenes 2→3→4. The block may not move, resize, or restyle across seams; the click promised in Scene 2’s narration is withheld until Scene 4’s first frame. That’s a real tension device, and it forces the “text is alive” claim to be literally seamless.
  • Deliberate visual rhyme: Scene 1’s locked cloud (data hostage) is compositionally inverted in Scene 7’s open sync arc (data yours) — same screen position, no padlock, specified on both ends. That’s the kind of idea that makes a 90-second film feel authored.
  • Consistency by constriction: one athlete, one hand, one cursor, one accent, one bell, one click ripple, one music bed, one mic setup; the per-render checklist operationalizes all of it. The character sheet’s “no smiling arcs, emotion is posture (4° slump)” keeps the tone from drifting into mascot territory.
  • Honest scope: Scene 1’s generic app is deliberately unstyled and never reused; Scene 2 shows no other fences or features — “simplicity is the shot.” The docs repeatedly choose saying less over showing off.

Where cohesion is thinner: Scene 5 is the only scene whose job is pure context rather than argument, and it’s the one scene whose central claims (finding C) rest on unaccepted hardware. It carries the film’s highest claim-risk per second of screen time.

4 · Missing elements — what the intro doesn’t say that the repo does

  1. The language’s breadth is left entirely on the table. The video shows exactly one sentence shape (rounds + timer + rest). The syntax reference also has AMRAP, EMOM, rep ladders (21-15-9), inline strength 5x5 Back Squat @100kg, choice groups 135/95 lb, wildcards ?, collectible timers :?, and lap/superset +. For the target visitor — someone whose training notes already look like this — “it parses what you already write” is the strongest unclaimed claim. A two-second ghost montage inside Scene 3’s existing fan-out beat would carry it without adding runtime.
  2. The pre-run wizard. Choice groups (Run/Row, 135/95 lb) trigger a pre-run wizard before the clock starts — the one moment the app asks the athlete something, and a genuine differentiator vs. static trackers. Absent. (It would also visually rhyme with Scene 1’s hated wizard — same object, opposite feel.)
  3. The log fence. Scene 4’s “nothing gets logged afterwards: execution is the logging” is the film’s boldest claim, and the language’s second fence — ```log, for recording actuals — is its unacknowledged escape hatch. One clause acknowledging planned vs. logged would make the claim honest instead of absolute.
  4. Editor assistance. The product ships Monaco with suggestions and semantic highlighting (per the repo). “It’s just text” undersells “and the editor helps you write it correctly.” Even the existing Scene 2 typed-gray → statement-black shift gestures at this; it could be named.
  5. Where the numbers in T-1 come from. Splits 14, 14, 13… are canonical fiction. Internally the docs are rigorous about this; a viewer will assume real recorded data. Not worth screen time, but worth a line in the video description/caption.
  6. Effort/movement registry. Efforts resolve against a registry (equipment, discipline, intensity, MET values) — it’s what makes effort:burpees filters and METScore analytics work. Invisible in the film; fine for 90 seconds, but it is the missing foundation under finding B’s METs problem.
  7. Embed-context fallback. The end card (“Keep scrolling — this is live ↓”) assumes the landing-page placement. On docs pages or social embeds the last beat dangles. Cheap: a variant end card without the scroll cue.
  8. Self-hosting one-liner. “Your own database” in Scene 7 gestures at wod server / the CLI (packages/tools, apps/api) without naming it. Defensible scope cut; noting it because it’s the cheapest trust win available in Scene 7’s storage beat.

5 · Recommended actions, in order

  1. Reword Scene 7’s publish line (finding A) before the narration session — it’s the only finding that changes recorded audio.
  2. Resolve finding B: chips to reps · load · distance · time · calories, drop METs from narration (also fixes D).
  3. Decide finding C (cast hardware risk) explicitly; record the decision in the outline’s locked-decisions list.
  4. Fix the doc-level contradictions and dead paths (E, G, J); declare one time convention (F).
  5. Resolve Scene 3’s four-annotation violation (I) — cheapest fix is app-styled highlights for load/distance.
  6. Decide UI-3’s source of truth (H): trace Session Detail, or ship in-note results first.
  7. Optional strengthener: the Scene 3 syntax-breadth montage (missing #1) — the highest-value 2 seconds the cut can add.
