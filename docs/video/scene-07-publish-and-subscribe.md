# Scene 7 — Publish & Subscribe
*Slot: 7 of 7 · Timeline 1:20–1:33 · Runtime 13s*

**Job of the scene:** the distributed close, as defined by Sergei 2026-10-20: training
knowledge is published as notebooks on GitHub for the community to subscribe to, while
your own data stays in your browser or your own database with Turso sync/backup. Then
break the fourth wall into the live demo.

## Narration script (32 words ≈ 13s)
*Delivery: warm, plain, final. Slow slightly for the last sentence — it's the handoff.*
> Publish a notebook to GitHub, and anyone can subscribe. Your data stays in your
> browser, or your own database — synced and backed up with Turso. Keep scrolling:
> the demo below is live.

- Storage chips (app-styled): **Browser** · **SQLite — yours** · **Turso sync**
- End card (Inter, app voice — not marker): **"Keep scrolling — this is live ↓"**

## Animation spec (second by second)
- **0:00–0:03** — Pull back from Scene 6's dashboard to the whole journal page. The
  page's notebook (the Tabata note and its collection) lifts as a card (UI-5); a
  GitHub mark and the word `Publish` appear on it as the Cursor clicks once
  (standard ripple). The card settles into a simple "published" shelf at board-left.
  *VO sync: "Publish a notebook to GitHub."*
- **0:03–0:06** — A second, plain laptop outline fades in at board-right (a community
  member's machine — a device, not a new character). The **Subscribe** button on the
  published card pulses; a copy of the notebook card travels across and lands in the
  second laptop, opening to the same Tabata page. Subscribing = receiving the same
  words. *VO sync: "and anyone can subscribe."*
- **0:06–0:10** — The view returns to *your* side: under your journal page, three
  storage chips assemble left-to-right: **Browser** (the journal page tucks into a
  browser window icon), **SQLite — yours** (a simple database cylinder on your own
  drive/machine outline), and a sync arc draws between them and a small cloud labeled
  **Turso sync** — an arc, not a vault: it moves copies, it doesn't hold your data
  hostage. (Composition rhymes with Scene 1's locked cloud, opposite meaning: no
  padlock anywhere.) *VO sync: "Your data stays in your browser, or your own
  database — synced and backed up with Turso."*
- **0:10–0:13** — The board is revealed as the top of a web page: browser chrome fades
  in at the top edge. The page nudges down a few dozen pixels; below the fold line,
  the real demo is visibly alive (a timer digit ticks). End card settles center:
  "Keep scrolling — this is live ↓". Hold 1.5s. End of film.
