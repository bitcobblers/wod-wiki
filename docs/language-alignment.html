Language Alignment · Working Proposal
Notebook, Feed, Note — one word per concept
A vocabulary proposal for the content domain: what a notebook is, what a feed is, what a note is — and which of today's words must retire to make those three unambiguous. Covers three aligned surfaces: the domain words, the URL prefixes, and the query language.
Grounded in: packages/core/src/types/notebook.ts · packages/core/src/types/storage.ts · packages/engine/src/seed/contract.ts · packages/tools/src/seedbuild/compiler.ts · packages/wql/src/QueryService.ts · apps/playground/src/services/seed/seedSources.ts · apps/playground/app/lib/entryMapper.ts · CONTEXT.md · docs/domain-model/

Read this first — the brief and the codebase disagree on one word.

The brief asks for notebook (sometimes called "collection or feed"), feed ("today known as seed"), and note. The codebase already uses feed for something else — a dated container (feed.<slug> chunks, markdown/feeds/, source:feeds) — while the concept the brief calls "feed" (a configured source of notebooks) is the codebase's seed (SeedSource, SEED_SOURCES, wodwiki.seedSources).

So "feed" cannot mean both. This document resolves it by renaming the codebase's dated container out of the word "feed" and giving "feed" to the source concept, per the brief — and flags the one alternative in §6 (D1) for a decision.

1. The proposal in one table

Three domain concepts, three canonical words. Everything else in the vocabulary either becomes one of these, becomes a qualifier of one of these, or retires.

ConceptCanonical wordDefinition (one sentence)Today this is calledAction
The container Notebook A named, addressable container of notes — notebook/{slug} — that holds notes and gives them their home. notebook, collection, catalog, feed, grouping, page (as a container) notebook — canonical. collection catalog feed (dated sense) retire.
The source Feed A configured, subscribable origin that publishes notebooks — one published seed directory, synced as one unit with one version and one checkpoint. seed, seed source, library, source, source entry, community feed seed → feed everywhere. source as a standalone noun retires (it collides with sourceId, sourcePath, and the generic "data source").
The entry Note A single piece of authored content inside a notebook — a workout, a dated log entry, a guide page, an effort definition, an equipment definition, a dashboard. note, entry, session, post, item, record, page (as a doc), definition note — canonical, absorbs session/post/item/entry. entry item retire as domain nouns.

Everything else follows from this table. The playground and the journal are notebooks (notebook/playground, notebook/journal). Equipment, guides, and efforts are notes — notes that happen to be definitions (they answer "what is a barbell?" rather than "what did I train today?"). A feed publishes notebooks; a notebook contains notes; a note contains blocks. No other nouns are needed to describe content.

The same three words then govern the other two surfaces. §8 gives each concept a URL prefix and a library list route; §9 gives each concept a WQL head and filter key. A concept is only "aligned" if all three surfaces use its word — otherwise the app still speaks three dialects of the same idea.

5. Why "feed" for the source is defensible

The brief's choice ("feeds … are all the configured sources for notebooks") is not arbitrary, and the survey supports it on three grounds.

1. It matches the everyday meaning

A feed is something that pushes items at you on a schedule. That is exactly what a seed source does: one published directory, polled on a version, delivering notebooks on subscription. Users already meet the word on the home screen ("Community Feeds" = DemoSeedSources) and in the DemoSeedSource type name.

2. It frees "catalog" and "collection"

Both currently name the container — and catalog has four unrelated extra meanings. Retiring them removes the largest glossary ambiguity in the repo, and the container keeps its clean name: notebook.

3. It separates origin from container

Today "feed" names a container that is a kind of container (dated). The source is not a container at all — it is a publisher. The word "feed" cleanly expresses the publisher relation, leaving container-ness to "notebook".

The cost, stated plainly

Every existing feed.* chunk id, markdown/feeds/ path, source:feeds WQL scope, FeedDetailPage, and ScriptFeed adapter must move — and the dated container needs a new name of its own (proposal: a dated notebook, expressed as a note date attribute, not a container kind). This is the single largest rename in the proposal.

6. Decisions I need from you

D1 — The word "feed". The brief says feed = configured source of notebooks, and the codebase's feed.* = dated container. Options:

  1. Recommended — brief wins, dated container is renamed. "feed" = source; feed.<slug> chunks and markdown/feeds/ become dated notebooks; dated-ness becomes a note attribute (note.date), not a container kind.
  2. Keep feed = dated container. Then the source concept needs another name — "source", "library", or "publisher" — and the brief's "feeds are the configured sources" is inverted.
  3. Split by tier. "feed" = source, and the dated container becomes its own noun entirely ("timeline", "program"), distinct from notebook. More vocabulary, one more concept to explain.

D2 — Corpus directory names. Today: markdown/collections/ (named) and markdown/feeds/ (dated), both compiler corpora; plus canvas/, dashboards/, efforts/, equipment/. Align to markdown/notebooks/? And should markdown/canvas/ become markdown/guides/ (fixing C10)?

D3 — "Page": container or placement? Page today groups notes by date or slug through PageNote, alongside Notebook. Under this proposal a note sits in exactly one notebook, and a page becomes a placement (which date, which page) — not a second container. Confirm, or keep pages as first-class containers (which keeps the two-container ambiguity C7 alive).

D4 — Efforts and equipment as notes. Confirmed as the intended model (§2), but they are referenced by name from inside workout blocks and have their own registries and stores. Is the target: (a) notes with kinds effort/equipment whose registries become queries over notes, or (b) notes for display, with the registries kept as a separate reference plane? (a) is the clean language; (b) is the cheap migration.

D5 — Does "note" stay user-owned? Today the glossary reserves "note" for user-owned content while the database stores every library row as a note. Under this proposal note = any content unit, and ownership is an attribute (feedId present ⇒ delivered, absent ⇒ authored; editability = "no feedId"). Confirm that wording, or keep "note" strictly for authored content and use a different word for delivered content.

D6 — The "playground journal" phrase. Read two ways: (a) the playground and the journal are both notebooks (the brief's grouping), or (b) a single thing named "playground journal" (a journal inside the playground). The codebase has no such combined object: notebook/playground and notebook/journal are separate default notebooks. I have assumed (a) — confirm.

D7 — The URL prefixes. §8 proposes /n/ notebook, /f/ feed, /d/ date, /p/ page, /effort/ effort, /eq/ equipment, /t/{type}/{tag} tag. Confirm the set, and confirm the mixed convention: three single letters (n, f, p), one two-letter (eq), one spelled out (effort). The alternative is all-spelled-out (/notebook/, /feed/, /page/) — unambiguous, but it collides with the list routes (/notebooks vs /notebook/) and makes URLs long.

D8 — Where do dashboards go? Resolved in this revision: /d/{slug} stays with dashboards and the date prefix moves to /j/{date}. The dashboard prefix was already in use and already correct, so moving the date prefix is the smaller change and avoids a redirect on a working route. /dashboards remains the dashboard list route, and /dashboard/:slug remains as the longer alias.

D8b — Is /j/ for "journal" or for "date"? The prefix reads as journal-day, which matches the concept today (the journal is the dated notebook). But under §2 the journal is just one notebook among several, and a dated view could plausibly exist for any notebook's notes — not only the journal's. Options: (a) /j/{date} — journal days only; (b) /n/{notebook}/{date} — a date scoped to any notebook, with /j/{date} as a shorthand for the journal notebook; (c) /day/{date} — a generic date prefix, honest about being a time slice rather than a place. (c) is what "date" means on its own; (a) is what the app has today.

D9 — How many notes can a page hold? The brief says "the page can be one or more notes". Today a page holds notes through the page_notes junction, and the calendar-page case holds one date's notes. Confirm that a page is a grouping with a slug (so /p/{slug} can list several notes), and that the journal-date case becomes /j/{date} rather than a page. This also decides whether pages keep their own table or become a notebook filter.

D10 — How far does WQL go? §9 proposes new heads :feed, :page, :tag, :equipment and new keys feed:, date:, tag:. Two sub-questions: (a) does :notebook{} become the notebook plane (reachable from every notebook, not just feed-delivered ones), or does it stay a scope alias over notes? (b) do /tags and /equipment become true query planes — new target enum entries with their own executors — or stay note-plane projections? A plane is the honest model and the composer supports it; a projection is cheaper and needs no new store seams.

8. URL language — the slug-addressable surface

The vocabulary has a third dimension the first two sections did not cover: how a thing is addressed. Today the URL surface encodes four unrelated prefix systems (/c/, /feeds/, /e/, /p/) plus list routes whose paths disagree with the titles they display (/catalogs shows the title "Notebooks", /feeds shows the title "Notes"). One prefix is already right and stays: /d/{slug} renders a dashboard, and a dashboard is a note of kind dashboard rendered as widgets — so d keeps dashboards and the date prefix moves to /j/.

The proposal: one prefix per concept, one list route per concept, no collisions. A single-letter prefix marks a direct rendering — a place you can land and read content. A plural noun marks a library list page — a queryable surface over many things. The two never share a prefix.

Reading a URL tells you the concept, the identifier, and whether you are looking at one thing or many. Today /feeds/dan-john-40-day/2026-01-12/session encodes four concepts in one path (feed, notebook, date, note) with no marker for which is which; under the proposal the same content is /f/dan-john-40-day → /n/dan-john-40-day → /j/2026-01-12 → /notes/<uuid>, each a link you can share on its own.

8.5 How the prefixes were chosen

Every prefix is one letter of the concept's own word, so the URL teaches the vocabulary instead of adding a lookup table:

PrefixWordCollision checkWhy this one
/n/notebookfreeFirst letter of notebook; the container is the thing most worth a short prefix because notebooks nest the most links.
/f/feedfreeFirst letter of feed; the source concept. Disambiguates from /feeds (the list) by case and by depth.
/j/journal dayfreeChosen over /d/ because /d/ is taken by dashboards. The date view in the app is the journal day, so j is the honest letter. See D8b if dated views should extend beyond the journal.
/p/pagefreeFirst letter of page; already in use and already correct.
/d/dashboardin useKeeps its current meaning. Dashboards are notes of kind dashboard rendered as widgets — a rendering of a note, exactly what /d/ already does.
/effort/effortfreeSpelled out because /e/ is one letter away from /eq/ and unreadable in isolation. The full word also matches the typed-tag key equipment: and the WQL head :effort.
/eq/equipmentfreeShort, and two letters from effort rather than one — enough separation that a mistyped URL fails visibly.
/t/tagfreeFirst letter of tag; two segments because a tag is only unique within its type.

The list routes are the plural of the concept; the prefixes are its initial. /notebooks lists notebooks, /n/ opens one. /feeds lists feeds, /f/ opens one. No list route and no prefix can be confused, because one is plural and the other is not.

9. WQL — the query language must carry the same vocabulary

If the words align but the query language does not, the alignment is cosmetic: a user who navigates to /n/crossfit-girls must be able to type the query that produced that view. The good news is structural — WQL's grammar is already open. Heads and filter keys are lexed as open words and validated semantically against closed enums, so a new head is a vocabulary + executor change, not a grammar rewrite.

9.2 The vocabulary WQL must gain

HeadMeaningShape of the change
:notebook{}Notes in any notebookToday this head means source:feeds + type:feed and takes the catalog path. It must become the notebook plane — including user notebooks and notebook/journal, notebook/playground, notebook/guides, which the current head cannot reach.
:feed{}FeedsToday :feed means source:feeds over notes. Under the new vocabulary a feed is a source — a list of notebooks. This needs a new plane, not a scope alias.
:page{}Pages (grouped notes)New. The page filter key already exists on the note plane; a head over pages does not. Note the precedent in reverse: find:page was retired on the grounds that "page-ness is handled by type:" — so reviving a :page head means reviving that decision, or keeping page-ness as a filter only.
:tag{}TagsNew plane. Tags are stored with a type and a label; the by-type index is the natural read.
:equipment{}Equipment notesNew plane. Under §2 equipment is a note kind, so this head returns equipment notes — and, once equipment is a note kind, the same head can also resolve the equipment store as a reference plane. The stores and the block_equipment containment index already exist; what is missing is the query seam, the target enum entry, and the dispatch arm.

Grammar impact: zero. The head rule is Head { colon Word } and the filter rule is Filter { Negate? TagKey colon TagValue } — both open words. A new head needs: an entry in the head vocabulary, a scope or type injection if it is a note-plane alias, or a new target plus a dispatcher arm. A new filter key needs: an executor arm, a capabilities registration (the composer refuses to advertise unregistered keys), a composer clause entry, and — if SQL push-down is wanted — a storage predicate and a domain-read compile step.

9.3 The filter keys that must follow the routes

  • feed:{slug} — notes delivered by a feed. Replaces catalog:, which is a retired word with six unrelated meanings.
  • notebook:{slug} — already exists and stays. Under the new vocabulary it filters on notebook/{slug} with no type injection.
  • page:{slug} — already exists on the note plane; keep it and make it work on the block plane too.
  • date:{YYYY-MM-DD} — new. Today dates are expressed by route or by a last 4w window suffix, never as a filter key. The key stays generic even though the route is journal-scoped: a note's date is a property of the note, so date: can filter any notebook's notes, while /j/{date} addresses the journal's day view (D8b).
  • tag:{type}:{slug} — new. The typed-tag executor already resolves (type, label) pairs on the note plane; a general tag: key makes it addressable from any surface, including /t/{type}/{tag}.
  • equipment:{slug} — exists as a typed tag but should be first-class once equipment is a plane.
  • catalog: — retire. It is the WQL surface's last holdout of the retired vocabulary.

9.4 The library-list contract

Every list route in §8.2 must satisfy the same contract, or the vocabulary is cosmetic:

  1. Bind a default query. The route carries a default WQL (:notebook{} on /notebooks), overridable by ?q=, overridable again by the user's saved config for that route — the precedence chain the stream profile already implements.
  2. Render WQL output, not a bespoke list. The list page renders whatever the query returns. /tags and /equipment today render hand-rolled lists and would have to stop.
  3. Expose the same filter set. Every list page's composer offers the filters that apply to its plane. The composer is already capability-driven — the allowed-clause set per target is declared in one place, so a new plane declares its own filters and inherits the rest.
  4. Round-trip. The query that produced a list is a shareable URL. /notebooks?q=:notebook{notebook:crossfit-girls} must be linkable, and the list must be reproducible from the URL alone.
  5. Link to the concept's own rendering. A row in /notebooks links to /n/{slug}; a row in /feeds links to /f/{slug}; a row in /tags links to /t/{type}/{tag}. The list and the prefix come from the same vocabulary.

10. What this proposal does not touch

  • Content and execution. Metrics, blocks, dialects, the runtime stack, and the parser are unaffected — they never spoke these nouns.
  • Results and sessions. Session (the execution log) and EventRecord are a different concept from the note kinds "session"/"post" and are not renamed here. The old entry-kind session retires; the storage entity Session survives.
  • WQL as a language. §9 changes its vocabulary — new heads and filter keys — not its grammar or its semantics. The head and filter rules are already open words; the parser, the pipeline model, the aggregator heads, the chart heads, the dataset references, and the cross-store where join are untouched.
  • The seed wire format's meaning. Manifest schema version and chunk contents keep their shape; only their names change.
  • Runtime and result URLs. /run/:runtimeId, /sessions, /results/:sessionId, and /session/:date address executions, not content. They keep their paths and are outside the content vocabulary — the Session entity survives the entry-kind retirement (§3.3).
  • Application and settings URLs. /settings/*, /subscribe, /load, and /proto/* are application chrome. /subscribe is the one that will want a new name once "seed" retires (it is the feed-subscription surface) — flagged for wave 5, not resolved here.

  • Source survey — every use of seed / source / registry / library / catalog as a source term, split by whether it means the source list, the import payload, or per-entry provenance.
